# Nov 7, 2026: designed the rest of the frame and printed it all

Yesterday I only had the fit-test plate and the middle plate. Today I designed the top plate, the bottom plate, the motor mount and the wheel, and got all of them printing at Thinkbox on two printers at once. I pick them up Monday.

The plan for every part is in [design/](../design/). This is what I actually did and what went wrong along the way.

## Part 1: top plate

The top plate holds the battery and has the TILT logo. I already had a plain copy of the plate base from yesterday (outline, corner holes, rounded edges), so I started from that.

**Cuts**
- 2 strap slots, 24 x 3 mm, 20.3 mm in front of and behind the center. A velcro strap goes through them and around the battery.
- A 10 x 5 mm hole next to the battery for the wires to go down, 47 mm right and 15 mm back.
- A row of 7 spare M3 holes along the back edge, 12 mm apart, for adding stuff later.

![Top plate slots](images/2026-11-07/01-top-plate-slots-sketch.jpg)

**The logo**

I used the Text tool to write TILT, 8 mm tall, in front of the battery. My first try was way too big and in the wrong spot. I didn't realize the top of the screen is the front of the robot, so it was sitting at the back.

![First try at the logo](images/2026-11-07/02-logo-first-try.jpg)

I also realized that if you stand in front of the robot and look down, the letters would be upside down. Drawing the text box backwards did NOT flip it. What worked was deleting the dimensions, using the **Transform** tool (I found it with Search tools) and rotating the text 180 degrees, then adding the dimensions back. So on screen it looks upside down, but on the robot it reads right.

![Logo flipped](images/2026-11-07/03-logo-flipped.jpg)

Then I added a "light bar" along the front edge: a 50 x 2.5 mm strip. It doesn't light up, it's just a stripe that shows which way is front. The logo and the light bar are raised 0.6 mm so they can print in a second color.

![Light bar](images/2026-11-07/04-light-bar-sketch.jpg)

For the spare holes I mixed up my two dimensions at first (40 sideways and 36 back instead of 36 sideways and 40 back), so the row was off center. I just swapped the numbers.

![Spare holes before I fixed them](images/2026-11-07/05-spare-holes-swapped.jpg)

## Part 2: bottom plate

The bottom plate holds the IMU, the buck converter, the motor driver, and both motor mounts. Before starting I asked for a picture of where everything goes, which helped a lot:

![Bottom plate plan](../design/images/parts/bottom-plate-plan.png)

Everything went in one sketch and got cut at once:
- **IMU:** 2 holes, 9.95 mm each side of center and 4.5 mm forward. It sits right over the axle because that spot moves the least when the robot rocks.
- **Motor mounts:** 2 holes on each side, 52 mm out from center, 1.75 and 11.75 mm back. I drew the right side and mirrored it.
- **Buck converter:** 2 short slots (4.3 x 3.3) instead of round holes, because my two measurements of its holes were about 1 mm apart.
- **Wire slots:** 16 x 6 on each side for the motor wires, and 22 x 6 up front for wires going to the ESP32.
- The same 7 spare holes as the top plate.

![Bottom plate sketch](images/2026-11-07/06-bottom-plate-sketch.jpg)

![Bottom plate cut](images/2026-11-07/07-bottom-plate-cut.jpg)

The motor driver has no mounting holes, so it doesn't get any. It'll stick down with foam tape, or go in a mini breadboard that's taped down. It moves to my PCB later anyway.

For a cool detail I added a raised arrow in the front right corner pointing forward, so it matches the logo color. I made it as a rectangle (8 x 6) plus a triangle (16 wide) and extruded both together. Trying to draw it with one line tool by clicking exact points didn't work because you can't see where your cursor is in mm.

![Bottom plate arrow](images/2026-11-07/08-bottom-plate-arrow.jpg)

## Part 3: motor mount

I was confused at first about how the motors actually attach to the plate, so I asked for a side view:

![How the motor mount works](../design/images/parts/motor-mount-side-view.png)

It's an L shape. The **wall** bolts flat to the motor's gearbox face, and the **tab** lies flat under the bottom plate. Two screws go down through the plate into the tab, so the motor hangs under the plate.

![Motor mount plan](../design/images/parts/motor-mount-3d.png)

Since the fit-test plate already fits perfectly, I duplicated it and changed it instead of starting over.

![Starting from the fit-test plate](images/2026-11-07/09-mount-from-fit-plate.jpg)

1. Deleted the round outline and drew a 44 x 40 rectangle around the holes. It goes 20 mm left and down from the center but 24 mm to the right. On this sketch, screen right is the robot's "up," so the extra room is where the tab goes.
2. Made it 4 mm thick.

![Mount wall sketch](images/2026-11-07/10-mount-wall-sketch.jpg)

3. Drew a 4 x 20 strip along the right edge and extruded it 20 mm to make the tab.

![Tab added](images/2026-11-07/11-mount-tab.jpg)

4. Put 2 holes in the tab, 2.8 mm (smaller than M3 on purpose so the screws thread into the plastic), 10 mm apart and 8.5 mm from the outside of the wall.

![Tab holes](images/2026-11-07/12-mount-tab-holes.jpg)

![Finished motor mount](images/2026-11-07/13-mount-done.jpg)

I only designed one. The other one is a mirror copy, which I did in the slicer.

## Part 4: wheel

The wheel needed a plan for grip, because a bare plastic wheel will slip. The wheel is 84 mm with a shallow groove around the edge, and a tire sits in the groove to bring it to 90 mm. Plan A is printing the tire in TPU (rubbery filament) if Thinkbox has it. Plan B is a strip of bike inner tube. Either way the wheel is the same.

I made it as 3 stacked circles: an 84 mm lip (1 mm), an 82 mm middle (10 mm), and another 84 mm lip (1 mm). That made the groove without any fancy tools.

![Lips and groove](images/2026-11-07/14-wheel-lips-and-groove.jpg)

Then I cut out a ring between 74 mm and 20 mm, 8 mm deep. That leaves an outer rim, a hub in the middle, and a 4 mm thick face. It saves plastic and looks way better.

![Cutout](images/2026-11-07/15-wheel-cutout.jpg)

Then 5 holes, 14 mm, using a circular pattern like I did on the fit-test plate. Now it looks like a car rim.

![Rim holes](images/2026-11-07/16-wheel-rim-holes.jpg)

Last was the shaft hole. The motor shaft has a flat side, so the hole is a D shape: a 6.2 mm circle with a straight line 2.75 mm from the center, and I trimmed off the small piece past the line. The flat stops the wheel from spinning on the shaft.

![D-shaped shaft hole](images/2026-11-07/17-wheel-d-hole.jpg)

## Printing

I printed in two batches on two printers at Thinkbox.

**Printer 1: top and bottom plates (PLA)**

The printer was a Bambu X2D, which has two nozzles, so I loaded black on one and turquoise on the other. All the raised parts (logo, light bar, arrow) start right where the 3 mm plate ends, so I added one filament change on the first layer above 3 mm in the slicer. Everything below prints black and everything above prints turquoise. I first thought I'd have to pause and swap the filament by hand, but with two nozzles it does it by itself.

![Both plates in the slicer](images/2026-11-07/18-slicer-two-plates.jpg)

![Logo in turquoise in the preview](images/2026-11-07/19-logo-slice-preview.jpg)

**Printer 2: 2 wheels and 2 motor mounts (PETG)**

I used PETG for these instead of PLA. PETG is tougher and bends a little instead of cracking, and the wheels and mounts take the hit every time it falls over while I'm tuning it (which will be a lot). Plates stay PLA because it's stiffer.

**Settings I changed for better quality**
- 0.16 mm High Quality layers instead of 0.2
- Ironing on top surfaces (smooth glossy tops)
- Seam position set to aligned (hides where each layer starts)
- Auto brim on the PETG parts so they don't lift
- Wheels and mounts: 4 walls and 40% infill, for strength

Changing the layer height moves the layers, so I had to re-check that the color change still landed on the first logo layer before printing.

## What I learned

- **Plan the part before drawing it.** Seeing a picture of where everything goes first made the sketches way faster.
- **Design the parts that depend on each other together.** The bottom plate holes only make sense once you know the motor mount.
- **Draw rough, then dimension.** I can't click exact spots, so I draw the shape about right and let dimensions put it in place.
- **Dimension to the blue lines, not loose points.** When I dimensioned to a point that could move, the holes moved instead of the rectangle.
- **Reuse what already works.** The motor mount started as the fit-test plate, so I didn't have to redo the hole pattern.
- **Raised details make an easy second color.** If everything colored starts at the same height, one filament change does it all.
- **PLA vs PETG:** PLA is stiff and easy, PETG is tougher. Pick based on what the part has to survive.

## Things I messed up (and what fixed them)

- **Front vs back.** I put the logo at the back because I forgot the top of the screen is the front.
- **Upside-down text.** Clicking the text box backwards didn't rotate it. The Transform tool did.
- **Swapped dimensions.** My spare hole row was off center because I typed the two numbers into the wrong dimensions.
- **Holes moving.** A dimension grabbed a point that wasn't locked and dragged the whole hole pattern. Undo, then dimension to the blue lines.
- **Top face vs Top plane.** "Sketch on the top face" means click the flat surface of the part, not "Top" in the feature list.
- **Color change menu.** The + on the slider only showed "Add Pause" until I added a second filament to the project.
