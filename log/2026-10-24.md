# Oct 24, 2026: motors tested, buck converter set to 5.0 V

All my parts showed up, so the last couple of days were about getting to know each one and making sure it works before I wire anything together.

## What I did

- **Tested both motors.** Motor 1 measured about 4.4 ohms. I spun each one in both directions on the lab supply, then did a stall test at 2 V for under half a second (about 0.49 A) so I wouldn't cook the motor. Both passed.
- **Checked the encoders.** They run on 3.3 V and the polarity matters. When I turned the shaft the reading flipped between 3.3 V and 0, so they work.
- **Soldered 4 wires onto the buck converter.** These are the first real solder joints for this project. I gave each one a tug to check it.
- **Set the buck converter to 5.0 V.** I fed it 7.4 V from the bench supply with a 0.5 A limit. It read 6.81 V at first and the screw didn't seem to do anything, but I was just turning it the wrong way. Once I went the other direction I got it to 5.01 V.

## What I learned

- A buck converter steps a higher voltage down to a lower one. The ESP32 can't take the battery's 7.4 V directly, so this goes in between.
- The current limit on a power supply protects whatever I plug in. If something shorts, the supply stops at 0.5 A. A LiPo battery would just keep pushing way more current, which is why I test on the supply first.
- I2C is how the ESP32 will talk to the IMU. If a device never answers with an ACK, the first thing to check is the wiring.
- If the buck converter gets stuck at one voltage, the screw is probably just at the end of its range. Turn it the other way.
